Workplace safety audits under OSHA 2007

Occupational Safety and Health Act, 2007 (Act No. 15 of 2007) · regulator: DOSHS · general guidance, not legal advice

If you run a workplace in Kenya, the Occupational Safety and Health Act, 2007 (OSHA) is the law that decides what you must do to keep staff safe and stay on the right side of the Directorate of Occupational Safety and Health Services (DOSHS). This guide covers the obligations that catch most businesses out, and the deadlines behind each one.

Who OSHA 2007 applies to

OSHA applies to virtually every "workplace" in Kenya where people are employed, from factories and construction sites to offices, shops, schools and hospitals. The duties fall on the occupier (usually the employer or the person in control of the premises).

The annual OSH audit, in detail

The audit is the obligation most businesses forget until an inspector appears. The law requires it at least once every 12 months, and it must be carried out by a safety and health advisor approved by DOSHS, not just anyone. The resulting report is submitted to DOSHS, and it should feed a corrective action plan that you actually work through before the next audit.

Alongside it sits the annual fire safety audit, carried out by an authorised fire safety auditor, which supports your fire safety compliance certificate. Many occupiers treat the OSH audit and fire audit as a single yearly exercise.

Training and the general register

Employers must provide information, instruction, training and supervision, and supply personal protective equipment (PPE) free of charge. You must also keep a general register and the records the Act requires. Refresher training should be documented and, for several hazard areas, delivered by DOSHS-approved training institutions.

What non-compliance costs

DOSHS inspectors can issue improvement notices and prohibition notices, and in serious cases order closure of the premises. Beyond notices, offences under the Act carry fines and the possibility of prosecution. The practical risk for most businesses is an enforcement visit that finds an expired audit or an unregistered workplace.

The surest way to avoid a notice is simple: know your renewal dates (registration, OSH audit, fire audit, WIBA) and act on your last audit's recommendations before the next one is due.
