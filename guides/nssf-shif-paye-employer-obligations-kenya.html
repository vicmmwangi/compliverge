Payroll compliance: NSSF, SHIF, PAYE & the Housing Levy

Tax Laws (Amendment) / Income Tax Act · NSSF Act 2013 · Social Health Insurance Act 2023 · Affordable Housing Act 2024 · general guidance, not tax advice

If you employ anyone in Kenya, four statutory deductions come off payroll every month: PAYE, NSSF, SHIF (which replaced NHIF) and the Affordable Housing Levy. Miss a remittance and penalties follow quickly. This guide lays out each one, the current rate, and the monthly deadline.

Rates and bands are set by law and revised from time to time. Always confirm the figure in force for the pay period against KRA, NSSF and the Social Health Authority before you run payroll.

PAYE (Pay As You Earn)

PAYE is income tax you withhold from employees' pay and remit to the Kenya Revenue Authority. It runs on graduated bands, from 10% on the lowest slice of monthly pay up to 35% on the highest, with a monthly personal relief applied to every resident employee. You file the PAYE return and pay by the 9th of the following month through iTax. Taxable pay includes benefits, so company housing, car and similar perks must be valued and added.

NSSF (National Social Security Fund)

Under the NSSF Act, 2013, contributions are 6% from the employee and a matching 6% from the employer, calculated on pensionable earnings and split into Tier I and Tier II against the lower and upper earnings limits. Those limits are gazetted and have been rising on a phased schedule, so the shilling amounts change periodically even though the 6% + 6% structure does not. Remit by the 9th of the following month.

SHIF (Social Health Insurance Fund)

SHIF, established under the Social Health Insurance Act, 2023, replaced the old NHIF. Employers deduct 2.75% of an employee's gross salary (subject to the prevailing minimum) and remit it to the Social Health Authority. Every employee must be registered. This is a straight percentage of gross, not a banded table like the old NHIF.

Affordable Housing Levy

Introduced by the Affordable Housing Act, 2024, the levy is 1.5% of gross monthly pay deducted from the employee, matched by 1.5% from the employer — 3% in total — and remitted to KRA by the 9th of the following month. It applies to gross pay with no upper cap.

Why the 9th matters

Most of these fall due on the 9th of the month after the pay period. Late remittance attracts penalties and interest from each authority, and PAYE in particular carries steep penalties for late filing and late payment, charged separately. For a small business, the risk is rarely not knowing the rate — it is losing track of the date across four different authorities.
