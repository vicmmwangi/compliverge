Environmental compliance with NEMA

Environmental Management and Co-ordination Act, 1999 (EMCA) · regulator: NEMA · general guidance, not legal advice

The National Environment Management Authority (NEMA) regulates how businesses in Kenya affect the environment, under the Environmental Management and Co-ordination Act, 1999 (EMCA). If you build, manufacture, process or discharge anything, NEMA's licences and audits apply to you. Here's what's required and when.

The Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) licence

Before you start a project listed in the Second Schedule of EMCA — new developments, factories, major construction, change of land use and more — you must carry out an Environmental Impact Assessment through a NEMA-registered expert and obtain an EIA licence. Starting a listed project without one is an offence, and NEMA can halt the project. The assessment covers the project's likely effects and the mitigation measures you commit to.

The annual environmental (self-auditing) audit

Once a facility is operational under an EIA licence, it must run an annual self-auditing environmental audit and submit the report to NEMA. This checks that you are actually following the Environmental Management Plan from your EIA and meeting the conditions of your licence. NEMA may also require an initial environmental audit after the project becomes operational.

Effluent discharge licences

If your operation discharges effluent — into a river, the ground, or a public sewer — you need a valid effluent discharge licence and must meet the water quality standards in the EMCA (Water Quality) Regulations. Licences are time-bound and must be renewed.

Waste, emissions and other controls

EMCA and its subsidiary regulations also govern waste management (handling, transport and disposal licences for certain waste), air quality and emissions, noise, and controlled substances. A manufacturing or industrial business usually holds several NEMA authorisations at once, each with its own renewal date.

What non-compliance costs

Operating without a required licence, or failing to submit audits, exposes a business to stop orders, improvement notices, fines and prosecution under EMCA. NEMA can issue environmental restoration orders requiring you to clean up and restore, at your own cost.

The pattern that trips businesses up is the annual audit: the EIA licence feels like a one-off, but the audit that keeps you compliant is due every single year.
